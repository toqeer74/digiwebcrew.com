The complaint is almost always the same: "We get decent traffic, the site looks good, and the phone doesn't ring."

For high-trust professional services — law firms, medical practices, consultancies, accountants — the cause is rarely the design. It's usually one of six things, and they're all fixable without a rebuild.

## 1. The site is about the firm, not the problem

Open your homepage and read the first heading. If it says something like "Excellence in Legal Representation Since 1998," it's addressed to you, not to the person reading it.

Someone landing on a family law page at 11pm is frightened and looking for a specific answer. The heading that converts them names their situation: "Divorce and custody, handled properly." Founding year and firm values matter later, on the About page, once they've decided you might be able to help.

**Fix:** Every page's first heading should name the visitor's problem or the outcome they want. Reserve credentials for the point where they're evaluating you, not discovering you.

## 2. There's one conversion path and it's the wrong temperature

Most professional services sites offer exactly one action: "Contact Us." That's a high-commitment ask aimed at people who are already convinced. Everyone else — the majority — leaves.

You need a low-commitment option running alongside it:

- "Book a free 15-minute consultation" instead of only "Request a Consultation"
- "See what a case like yours typically involves"
- "Download the checklist we give new clients"

**Fix:** Give every page a high-intent action *and* a low-intent one. The low-intent path is where most of your pipeline actually comes from.

## 3. Service pages are too shallow to rank or convince

A 200-word page called "Personal Injury" does two things badly. It doesn't rank, because a competitor has 2,000 words answering real questions. And it doesn't convince, because it answers none of the things a worried person needs to know.

The pages that work cover: what this actually involves, how long it typically takes, what it costs or what shapes the cost, what happens first, and what makes your firm's handling different.

**Fix:** Pick your three highest-value services and write them properly. Depth on three beats thinness on twelve. This is also the single highest-return [SEO](/services/seo-growth-retainers) work available to most professional services firms.

## 4. No proof anywhere near the decision point

Testimonials sitting in a carousel on the homepage do almost nothing. Proof works when it's adjacent to the moment of doubt.

A relevant result placed directly beside the enquiry form on the specific service page — "we handled 40 cases like this last year, average resolution 5 months" — carries far more weight than a rotating quote three scroll-lengths away.

Regulated professions have real constraints here, and you must follow your bar or medical board rules. But "we can't publish testimonials" doesn't prevent publishing case volume, years of experience in the specific area, or process transparency.

**Fix:** Move proof next to the ask. One relevant, specific piece beats five generic ones.

## 5. Forms ask for too much, too early

A nine-field form asking for case details before you've earned any trust converts badly. Every field is a reason to leave.

For a first touch you need enough to make contact and route correctly. Usually: name, one contact method, and a single sentence about the situation. Everything else is intake, and intake happens after they've raised their hand.

**Fix:** Cut the first form to three fields. Move the rest into a follow-up. Expect a meaningful lift — form-length effects here are large and well-documented.

## 6. Mobile is an afterthought where the traffic is

For most professional services firms, the majority of traffic is mobile, and it converts worse. Usually because of specifics:

- The phone number isn't tappable
- The form is below three screens of content
- Buttons are too small or too close together
- The page is slow on a real phone on a real network (see [Core Web Vitals](/blog/core-web-vitals-guide))

**Fix:** Open your own site on your phone, on cellular data, and try to make an enquiry. Time it. This exercise finds more problems in five minutes than most audits.

## The order to fix these in

If you do nothing else, do these three, in this order:

1. **Rewrite the headline and primary call to action on your top three service pages.** Cheap, fast, biggest immediate effect.
2. **Cut the enquiry form down.** One afternoon of work.
3. **Add a low-commitment conversion path.** A short consultation booking beats a contact form for people who aren't ready to commit.

Then measure for a month before doing anything structural. Most firms discover they didn't need the rebuild they were about to commission.

## When it is the build

Sometimes the site genuinely is the problem — it's slow, it can't be edited, the CMS fights you, or the structure can't support the content depth you need. At that point patching costs more than replacing.
