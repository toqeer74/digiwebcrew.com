Nexus Legal came to us with a site that looked dated next to the calibre of work the firm actually did. Prospective clients were landing on a generic homepage, struggling to understand which practice areas applied to them, and leaving without making contact.

## The challenge

The existing site treated every practice area the same. A commercial litigation client and a private client seeking estate planning saw identical messaging, identical imagery, and the same generic "Contact Us" form buried three clicks deep.

That created two compounding problems. Search engines had nothing specific to rank — one thin page could not compete for a dozen distinct practice-area searches. And the enquiries that did arrive were poorly qualified, so partners were spending billable hours on calls that were never going to convert.

## Our approach

We started by mapping how prospective clients actually arrive at a law firm. Almost nobody searches for "law firm." They search for the problem they have, in the city they are in.

That reframing drove the whole information architecture. Instead of one firm-level story, we built a page per practice area, each written for a specific problem and a specific reader, with the attorneys who handle that work surfaced directly on the page.

## What we built

Each practice area became a first-class landing page with its own explanation, relevant attorney bios, representative matters, and a consultation request path that stays visible as the reader scrolls.

Behind that, we rebuilt the front end on Next.js for speed and moved content into Sanity so the marketing team can publish new practice areas without a developer. Technical SEO work — clean semantic structure, schema markup, and a genuinely fast page — gave each of those pages a real chance to rank.

## The outcome

Consultation requests more than doubled, and the practice-area pages now rank on page one for their target terms. Just as importantly, the intake team reports that inbound enquiries arrive far better qualified, because the reader has already self-selected into the right practice area before they ever fill in a form.
