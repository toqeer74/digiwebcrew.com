Peak had four clinic locations and almost no organic visibility. Nearly every new patient came through paid ads, which made growth expensive and, more importantly, fragile.

## The challenge

The site had one combined "locations" page listing four addresses, no service-level content, and inconsistent business listings across the web. Search engines had very little reason to surface any individual clinic for any individual treatment.

The commercial risk was the real issue. With acquisition running almost entirely through paid channels, the cost of a new patient was set by the ad auction, and any pause in spend meant an immediate drop in bookings.

## Our approach

Local healthcare search is won at the intersection of location and treatment — someone searches for a specific problem, near where they live. That means the unit of content is not the clinic and not the service, but the combination of both.

We also treated this as an ongoing programme rather than a project. Local rankings compound, and the work only pays off if it continues past launch.

## What we built

We gave each location its own page with proper local markup and genuinely location-specific content, then built service pages around the treatments patients actually search for rather than the clinical names the team used internally.

We cleaned up business listings across the web so name, address, and hours matched everywhere, then moved into a continuing cycle of content, technical fixes, and measurement.

## The outcome

Organic sessions grew 280% year over year, every location now holds a top-three local pack position, and organic bookings have quadrupled. The clinic's dependence on paid acquisition has dropped substantially, which has changed how they plan and budget for growth.
